Anything a person can do in the editor by dragging, they can also do from the keyboard. This page lists the keys, so you can pass them on to the people using your app.

## Selecting a Block

<kbd>Tab</kbd> moves into the email and selects a Block. The arrow keys then go
from Block to Block. The email is one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, at the selected
Block, so the other Blocks aren't stops: <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes from the selected
Block to its text and its toolbar, then past the toolbars of the Rows and empty
Columns after it, to Preview and the side panel, which shows that Block's
settings.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Reaches the selected Block, or with nothing selected the first, and selects it. From there, <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes into its text, if it has any, then to its toolbar, and then on past the other Blocks, stopping only at the toolbars of the Rows and empty Columns after it, to the side panel and the Block's settings. |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Selects the Block before or after this one, in reading order. This reaches every Block in the email. |
| <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | Selects a Block in the next Column that has one, as close as it can get to the same position. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Goes into the Block's text to edit it. Held down, it goes in and stops, with no empty paragraphs added. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Comes back out of the text to the Block. |

Selecting a Block changes nothing in the email, so it doesn't fire `mq-change`.

## Moving a Block

Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> (Option on a Mac) and press an arrow key to move the selected Block. The same keys work while focus is in the Block's toolbar.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Moves the Block up or down its Column. Past the end of the Column, it moves into the next Row. Past the top or bottom of the email, it gets a Row of its own, unless it's alone in its Row already, when it stays and the editor says it can't move. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | Moves the Block into the next Column, including an empty one. It stops at the edge of its Row. |

Each move is one step for undo, and focus stays on the Block, so the next press moves it again.

Inside a Block's text, the arrow keys move the cursor instead, and <kbd>Alt</kbd> with an arrow key moves it by a word on macOS. Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> first to move the Block.

On Windows and Linux, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Right</kbd> also go back and forward a page in the browser. Nowhere in the editor do they do that, so a press meant to move a Block never leaves your page.

## The Block's toolbar

A selected Block has a toolbar with a drag handle, four move buttons, Duplicate Block and Delete Block. It's one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, and the arrow keys go across its buttons. <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last button. <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the Block, once it has closed the tooltip of the button with focus. A tooltip that shows because the pointer is over a button closes with any <kbd>Escape</kbd>, which still does what it would have done.

## Rows

A Row's toolbar comes just before the Row's Blocks, so <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> from the Row's first Block reaches it. It has a drag handle, Select Row, the two Move Row buttons, Duplicate Row and Delete Row. Like the Block's toolbar, it's one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, the arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last.

Select Row shows the Row's settings in the side panel. While a Row is selected, no Block is a stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, so <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes on past the toolbars of the Rows and empty Columns after it to those settings.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | In the Row's toolbar, moves the Row up or down with its Blocks, as one step for undo. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | In a Row's toolbar while a Row is selected, unselects that Row and selects the first Block of this toolbar's Row, with focus on it. If this toolbar's Row has no Blocks, nothing is selected. As in the Block's toolbar, it first closes the tooltip of the button with focus. |

## Duplicating and deleting

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> | On a Block or in its toolbar, puts a copy of the Block right after it, in the same Column. In a Row's toolbar, puts a copy of the Row, with its Blocks, right after it. The copy is selected, and focus goes to it, or for a Row, to its toolbar. The browser doesn't bookmark the page. |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | On a Block, deletes it. In a Row's toolbar, deletes the Row. Focus moves to the Block after it, or before it if it was the last, and for a Row to the next Row's toolbar, or the previous one's. |

These do what the Duplicate and Delete buttons in the toolbars do, and each is one step for undo. In a Block's text or a settings field, the keys edit the text as usual, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> is left to the browser. A Row whose Columns aren't one of the layouts' widths, as an email made outside the editor can have, is copied with even Columns, as many as it has.

## Copying, cutting and pasting

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> | On a Block, copies it. In a Row's toolbar, copies the Row with its Blocks. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>X</kbd> | Cuts: copies the Block or Row, then deletes it as <kbd>Delete</kbd> does, and focus moves the same way. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> | On a Block, in a Row's toolbar, or in the toolbar below the email, pastes. What's pasted is selected, with focus on it, or for a Row on its toolbar. A pasted Block, Row or text is one step for undo, and image files are added as dropped ones are. |

The browser's Edit menu does the same. A paste goes where this table says. In a Row's toolbar, that Row counts as selected.

| What's pasted | With a Block selected | With a Row selected | With nothing selected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Block copied in a Mail Quilt editor | After it, in its Column | In a new Row after it | In a new Row at the end |
| A Row copied in a Mail Quilt editor | After the Block's Row | After it | At the end |
| Text or HTML from another app | A new Text Block after it | A new Text Block in a new Row after it | A new Text Block in a new Row at the end |
| An image file | A new Image Block after it | A new Image Block in a new Row after it | A new Image Block in a new Row at the end |

A Block or Row pasted from a Mail Quilt editor, in this email, another one, or another tab, keeps every setting, and gets new IDs. Pasted anywhere else, such as in a document, it's text and simple HTML: text keeps its formatting and Lists, an Image is an image, and a Button is a link. Text or HTML from another app keeps only what a Text Block can hold, as when it's pasted into text, and each `{{name}}` in it becomes a Merge Tag. An image file uploads as a dropped one does, as [Upload images](./upload-images#pasting-an-image) says. Without `uploadImage` it's refused, and screen readers hear that the editor can't upload images. A copy from a newer version of the editor is refused, and screen readers hear why.

In a Block's text or a settings field, these keys copy, cut and paste text as usual. A Block pasted into text gives its text.

## Adding a Block

The side panel's tabs, Content, Rows and Settings, are one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>. The arrow keys open the next or previous tab, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> the first or last.

In the side panel's Content tab, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on a Block to add it after the selected Block, in the same Column. With a Row selected instead, it goes to the end of that Row's first Column. With nothing selected, it goes to the end of the email's last Column.

An empty Column has a toolbar named for where it is, such as "Add a Block to Row 1, Column 2", with a button for each type of Block, such as "Add Text Block here". Like the Block's toolbar, it's one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, the arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last. <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds that Block to the Column.

## Adding a Row

In the side panel's Rows tab, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on a layout to add a Row with it after the selected Row, or after the Row of the selected Block. With nothing selected, it goes at the end of the email.

## Settings

A settings field, such as a Button's link or a Row's padding, changes the email when you press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or leave the field. Until then, <kbd>Escape</kbd> takes back what you typed and shows the email's value again. A value the editor refuses stays in the field with the reason, until you fix it or press <kbd>Escape</kbd>.

A Social Block's links are a list in its settings, each with its URL field and a toolbar of Move up, Move down and Remove. The toolbar comes before the URL, and like the Block's toolbar it's one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>: the arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last. After a move, focus stays on the button that moved the link. After a removal, it goes to the Remove button of the next link, or of the one before if it was the last, or to the list of networks if none is left. To add a link, choose a network that isn't in the list yet and press Add, and focus goes to the new link's URL. What's typed in a URL stays with its link when links move or are removed.

## Cancelling a drag

While a Block or Row is dragged with the pointer, including a new one from the side panel, <kbd>Escape</kbd> cancels the drag. Nothing moves and nothing is added. Until the drag ends, the keys that select or move a Block or Row, or go into a Block's text, do nothing.

## Editing text

<kbd>⌘</kbd> is for a Mac, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> for everything else. On a Mac,
Ctrl+K and Ctrl+Y keep their usual meaning in text: delete to the end of the
line, and put it back.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Starts a new paragraph. In a List's item, it starts a new item, and in an empty item it leaves the List, splitting it if the item was in the middle. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Adds a line break in the same paragraph or item. |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Deletes backward. At the start of a List's item, it makes the item a paragraph, above the List if it was the first item and otherwise splitting the List. At the start of the paragraph just after a List, it joins the paragraph to the List's last item. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd> do the same, and on a Mac so do <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd> and <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>. |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> | Deletes forward. In an empty paragraph, it removes the paragraph, even the last one in the Block, and in an empty item it removes the item. At the end of a List's last item, or of the paragraph before a List, it pulls in the next paragraph's text, or the next List's first item, and that List keeps its type. When a paragraph between two Lists of the same type goes, with either key, they become one List, numbered on. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> does the same, and on a Mac so do <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Also adds a line break. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>B</kbd> | Turns bold on or off for the selected text. A Heading is bold already, so it does nothing there. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>B</kbd> does the same. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd> | Turns italic on or off. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd> does the same. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>U</kbd> | Turns underline on or off. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>U</kbd> does the same, so that on Windows and Linux the browser doesn't format the text in its own way. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>8</kbd> | Makes each selected paragraph an item of a bulleted List, in a Text Block. In a bulleted List already, it makes the selected items paragraphs again, and in a numbered List it makes the whole List bulleted. It's the key with 8 on it, even on a keyboard where Shift with it types something other than `*`. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>7</kbd> | The same for a numbered List. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> | Opens the link field for the selected Block's text, from anywhere in the email or its toolbars, such as the text, the Block or its toolbar. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it and leaves the text as it was. With no text to link, such as on a Button, the key is left to your page. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>F10</kbd> | Goes to the formatting toolbar, for bold, italic, underline, bulleted and numbered Lists, links, line breaks and, when your app gives the editor its Merge Tags, the Merge Tag button. The arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last. <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the text, once it has closed the tooltip of the button with focus. |

## Picking a Merge Tag

When your app gives the editor its [Merge Tags](./api#merge-tags), typing `{{` in a Block's text opens a list of them at the cursor, and what's typed after `{{` narrows it. The format bar's Merge Tag button types `{{` for you. Focus stays in the text, and a screen reader is told the highlighted tag as it changes. Typing `{{` in a settings field that holds text or a URL, or in the link field, opens the same list under the field, with the same keys.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Highlights the tag before or after, going round from either end. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Puts in the highlighted tag, in place of the `{{` and what was typed after it. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Closes the list and leaves the text as typed. The next <kbd>Escape</kbd> comes back out of the text to the Block, takes back what was typed in a settings field, or closes the link field. |

In a field, <kbd>Enter</kbd> puts in the tag as the text `{{name}}` without applying the field, and the next <kbd>Enter</kbd> applies it. Every other key does what it does in text, or in the field, so typing goes on. In text, typing `}}` makes a tag of what was typed, listed or not. Leaving the text or the field closes the list too.

## Undo and redo

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Undoes the last change. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Redoes it. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Y</kbd> | Also redoes it, except on a Mac. |

With nothing to undo or redo, these keys are left to your page.

## Keys and your page

The keys the editor acts on stop in the editor, so your page doesn't act on them too. Every other key goes on to your page, including what's typed in a Block's text or a settings field, as from any field on a page. The link field is the one exception: it keeps every key to itself.

The editor is inside a shadow root, so your page sees a key pressed in it as coming from the `<mail-quilt>` element, or from `<mail-quilt-shell>` in the Shell. A shortcut of your page's own, such as a letter that opens search, can skip those keys:

```js
document.addEventListener("keydown", (event) => {
  if (event.target.closest("mail-quilt, mail-quilt-shell")) {
    return;
  }
  // Your page's own shortcuts.
});
```

## Preview

Opening Preview moves focus to its Close button. Before Close, Desktop and Phone choose the width the email is shown at, and the choice stays while the editor is open. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it, and focus returns to where it was. In Safari, <kbd>Escape</kbd> does nothing once focus is inside the email, so press <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> to get back to Close.

## What a screen reader announces

- **A Block's place.** Each Block's name says where it is, such as "Text Block, Row 1, Column 2, position 1 of 3".
- **Every move.** After a Block or Row moves, the editor says where it went. When one can't go further, it says that instead.
- **Undo and redo of a move.** These say where the Block went back to.
- **Duplicating.** After a Block or Row is duplicated, the editor says where the copy went, such as "Block duplicated, Row 1, Column 1, position 2 of 3".
- **Copying, cutting and pasting.** The editor says what was copied or cut and where it was, or where what was pasted went, such as "Block pasted, Row 2, Column 1, position 3 of 3". A paste it refuses says why.
- **A Social Block's links.** After a link is added, moved or removed in the settings, the editor says so, such as "Facebook moved to position 2 of 3".
- **Deleting.** After a Block or Row is deleted, the editor says so. Focus moves to the Block after it, or before it if it was the last. For a Row, focus moves to the next Row's tools, or the previous Row's.
- **Messages in the Shell.** The [Shell](./shell) announces each message in its bar, such as an email it couldn't open.

## How it's tested

The editor's browser tests press these keys in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. They also run [axe-core](https://github.com/dequelabs/axe-core) with its WCAG 2.2 AA rules against the editor in several states: with Columns and empty Columns, with a Block selected, with the link field showing an error, with the Merge Tag picker open in text and under a settings field, with a Social Block's links showing a refused URL, and with Preview open at each width.

Automated checks don't find everything. The [accessibility statement](./accessibility) says which WCAG 2.2 criteria the editor meets, what it doesn't, and how each was checked. If a screen reader or another tool has trouble with the editor, please [open an issue](https://github.com/mailquilt/editor/issues).
