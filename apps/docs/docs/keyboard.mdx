Anything a person can do in the editor by dragging, they can also do from the keyboard. This page lists the keys, so you can pass them on to the people using your app.

## The top bar and the Modes

The top bar is above the email in every Mode. It holds Undo and Redo, the three Modes, Edit, Style and Check, and the Desktop and Phone views. In the [Shell](./shell), it also holds the email's name, whether it's saved, More and Export.

The Modes are one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, at the Mode you're in. The arrow keys go to the next or previous Mode and open it, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> the first or last. With nothing in the email, Style and Check are off. The arrow keys still reach them, and their tooltip says why. Check shows the email at desktop and phone width together, so it has no Desktop and Phone buttons.

When Undo or Redo has nothing left to do, focus goes to the other one, or if neither works, to the toolbar below the email, or in Style and Check to the Mode you're in.

In the Shell, Export and More each open a menu. <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Down</kbd> opens it with focus on its first item, and <kbd>Up</kbd> on its last. The arrow keys go along its items, <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> to the first and last, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> chooses one. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes the menu and goes back to its button, and goes no further, so a dialog of your page's around the Shell stays open. <kbd>Tab</kbd>, or a press anywhere else, closes it too.

## On a phone

Below {{editor-phone-width}} pixels wide, such as on a phone or at 400% zoom, the top bar holds Undo, the Modes and a More button, which opens a menu of the rest: Redo, Desktop or Phone, the Rows list, and in the Shell, its name, how what's not on this device shows, Export and Import. The Selection bar is docked at the bottom, and the keys on this page do what they do on a laptop. It keeps a Block's main setting and its color, and More has the rest, starting with Move up, Move down, Duplicate and Delete. With nothing selected, its Email chip opens the email's own settings. While a Block's text is being typed in, the format bar is at the bottom instead, and Done in the top bar goes back to the Block, as <kbd>Escape</kbd> does.

The Add panel, More, the Selection bar's popovers and its chip's menu, the top bar's menu, the Rows list and the link field open as sheets from the bottom of the screen. The chip's menu starts with what's selected, and its arrow keys go along its items as on a laptop. Focus goes into a sheet and stays there, as in any modal dialog: <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> go round what's in it. Done, <kbd>Escape</kbd> or a press on the dimmed email closes it, and focus goes back to the button that opened it. A sheet opened from another has a back arrow too, and closes back to the first. Where <kbd>Escape</kbd> does something else first, such as closing a tooltip or taking back a value a field refused, a second <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes the sheet. In the Rows list, each Row has buttons to move it up and down, and pressing a Row selects it and closes the list. Style's panel and Check's Problems stay at the bottom, under the email, and Style's Done goes back to Edit. Style shows one section at a time, Colors, Text or Button corners, chosen by the buttons at its top.

## Selecting a Block

<kbd>Tab</kbd> moves into the email and selects a Block. The arrow keys then go
from Block to Block. The email is one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, at the selected
Block, so the other Blocks aren't stops: <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes from the selected
Block to its text, then past the buttons of any empty Columns after it, to the
Selection bar above the email, which has that Block's settings, and then to the
button that opens Check.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Reaches the selected Block, or with nothing selected the first, and selects it. From there, <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes into its text, if it has any, and then on past the other Blocks, stopping only at the buttons of empty Columns after it, to the Selection bar and the Block's settings. |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Selects the Block before or after this one, in reading order. This reaches every Block in the email. |
| <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | Selects a Block in the next Column that has one, as close as it can get to the same position. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Goes into the Block's text to edit it. Held down, it goes in and stops, with no empty paragraphs added. A Block folded as it isn't on the device being viewed has no text drawn, so Enter does nothing there; switch to the view it shows on to edit it. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Comes back out of the text to the Block. |

Selecting a Block changes nothing in the email, so it doesn't fire `mq-change`.

## Moving a Block

Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> (Option on a Mac) and press an arrow key to move the selected Block. The same keys work while focus is on a button in the Selection bar.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Moves the Block up or down its Column. Past the end of the Column, it moves into the next Row. Past the top or bottom of the email, it gets a Row of its own, unless it's alone in its Row already, when it stays and the editor says it can't move. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | Moves the Block into the next Column, including an empty one. It stops at the edge of its Row. |

Each move is one step for undo, and focus stays on the Block, so the next press moves it again. In a view that doesn't show every Row, such as the Phone view with a Row that's only on desktop, a Block moved into a Row the view folds or hides leaves what the email shows, and focus goes to the toolbar below the email. Switch to the view that shows the Row to go on moving it.

Inside a Block's text, the arrow keys move the cursor instead, and <kbd>Alt</kbd> with an arrow key moves it by a word on macOS. Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> first to move the Block.

On Windows and Linux, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Right</kbd> also go back and forward a page in the browser. Nowhere in the editor do they do that, so a press meant to move a Block never leaves your page.

## The Selection bar

Above the email, the Selection bar has what's selected: a Block's main settings and its Move up, Move down, Duplicate and Delete buttons, a Row's, or with nothing selected the email's own settings. It starts with a chip that says what's selected and where, such as "Row 3 / Button", and ends with More, for the rest.

The bar is one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, at its chip, or with nothing selected its first field. The arrow keys go across its controls, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last. In a field, such as a Button's link, <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Right</kbd> move the cursor, and go on to the control before or after the field from either end of its text. A button that can't be used, such as a decorative Image's Link, stays reachable and says why in its tooltip. <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the selected Block, once it has closed the tooltip of the control with focus. A tooltip that shows because the pointer is over a control closes with any <kbd>Escape</kbd>, which still does what it would have done.

The chip opens a menu of what's around what's selected: a Block's Row and Column, or a Row's Columns, and the email. <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens it with focus on its first item. <kbd>Down</kbd> doesn't, as it goes along the bar. In the menu, the arrow keys go along its items, <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last, <kbd>Enter</kbd> chooses one, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it and goes back to the chip. Choosing the Row or the Column selects it, and choosing the email selects nothing, so the bar shows the email's settings.

A setting with more than one field, such as a Row's padding or a color, opens a popover under its button, with focus in its first field. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it and goes back to its button. <kbd>Tab</kbd> out of it, or a press anywhere else, closes it too.

More opens a panel beside the email where there's room for it, and over the email where there isn't. From the keyboard, focus goes to its first field, or its first action. It has the settings the bar doesn't show, and for a Block its Box: Padding and Border each a button that opens their fields in a popover, as the bar's do, and Background and Corners as fields. For a Block or a Row, Show on is a row of three buttons, All devices, Desktop only and Phones only, and pressing one sets it. Then come Copy, Cut and Paste below, and for a Block, Move to the Column before, Move to the next Column and Select its Row. A Column's More has only Select its Row. One that can't be done, such as moving into the next Column from the only one, stays reachable and says why in its tooltip. Where the bar is short of room, its Move, Duplicate and Delete buttons are in More instead. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes More and goes back to its button. It stays open as what's selected changes, and shows that instead.

## Columns

A Column is selected from the keyboard by the Selection bar's chip, whose menu names a Block's Column, or a Row's Columns, empty ones too, or with the pointer by a press on the Column's own space, beside or below its Blocks. With a Column selected, the Selection bar has its background, padding, alignment and border. A Column moves, copies and is deleted only with its Row, so it has none of those keys, and nothing is pasted with a Column selected.

While a Column is selected, no Block is a stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, as while a Row is, so <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> leave it selected.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | In the Selection bar, unselects the Column and selects its first Block, with focus on it. If the Column has no Blocks, its Row is selected, and focus stays in the bar. As with a Block, it first closes the tooltip of the control with focus. |

## Rows

A Row is selected by its tag, which shows above its end while the pointer is over the Row, from the Rows list beside the email, or from the keyboard by the Selection bar's chip, whose menu names the Block's Row. A Row whose Columns are all empty has no Block to start from, so focus on one of its Columns' buttons selects it. With a Row selected, the Selection bar has its layout, its padding and its Move Row, Duplicate Row and Delete Row buttons.

While a Row is selected, no Block is a stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, so <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes on past the buttons of the empty Columns after it to the Selection bar.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | In the Selection bar, moves the selected Row up or down with its Blocks, as one step for undo. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | In the Selection bar, unselects the Row and selects its first Block, with focus on it. If the Row has no Blocks, nothing is selected, and focus stays in the bar, which shows the email's settings. As with a Block, it first closes the tooltip of the control with focus. |

### The Rows list

The Rows list, before the email, shows each Row as a small picture, or collapsed, as its number. Its button at the top collapses it or expands it, and it stays that way while the email is open. Each Row is named with its Position and what it holds, such as "Row 3 of 6: Heading, Text, Button". The Row that's selected, or that holds the selected Block, is marked as the current one.

The list's Rows are one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>: the current Row, or else the first. Focus on a Row doesn't select it, and its ring sits further out than the current Row's, so the two are told apart. While one has focus, its tooltip says its Position and the keys that move it. A Row that isn't on phones has a crossed-out phone on its picture, and "Desktop only" in its name.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Goes to the Row before or after. <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Selects the Row, so the Selection bar shows its settings, and scrolls the email to it. Focus stays on the Row in the list. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Moves the Row with focus up or down with its Blocks, as one step for undo, and selects it, so the Selection bar shows what moved. Focus stays on it. |

With the pointer, a click selects a Row the same way, and dragging a Row's picture moves it. A Row dragged on the email can be dropped on the list too.

The list's + opens the layouts of an empty Row, with focus on the first. The arrow keys go along them, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds an empty Row with that layout after the current Row, or at the end with nothing selected, as the layouts say under them. Where your app offers [Saved Rows](./api#saved-rows), they're after the layouts, one more stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd> with the arrow keys along them, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds a copy of one in the same place. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes them and goes back to the +.

An email with no Rows says "Start with a Row" on the email, with the same layouts, one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, and <kbd>/</kbd> there opens the Add panel.

Where your app offers [Starter emails](./api#starter-emails), a new email opens on a start screen instead. Each card, Blank first, is its own stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> picks it. After Blank, focus goes to the first layout of "Start with a Row". After a Starter email, the editor says "Started from" its name, and focus goes to the toolbar below the email, or on a phone to the Edit tab, as it does when a new email opens.

## Duplicating and deleting

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> | On a Block or in the Selection bar, puts a copy of the selected Block right after it, in the same Column, or with a Row selected, a copy of the Row, with its Blocks, right after it. The copy is selected, and focus goes to it, or for a Row, stays in the bar, which shows the copy. The browser doesn't bookmark the page. |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | On a Block, deletes it. In the Selection bar with a Row selected, deletes the Row. Focus moves to the Block after it, or before it if it was the last. For a Row, the next Row is selected, or the one before if it was the last, and focus stays in the bar. Held down, it deletes only the one, and the next press deletes the next. |

These do what the Duplicate and Delete buttons in the Selection bar do, and each is one step for undo. In a Block's text or a settings field, the keys edit the text as usual, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd> is left to the browser. A Row whose Columns aren't one of the layouts' widths, as an email made outside the editor can have, is copied with even Columns, as many as it has.

## Copying, cutting and pasting

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> | On a Block, copies it. In the Selection bar with a Row selected, copies the Row with its Blocks. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>X</kbd> | Cuts: copies the Block or Row, then deletes it as <kbd>Delete</kbd> does, and focus moves the same way. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> | On a Block, in the Selection bar outside its fields, or in the toolbar below the email, pastes. With a Column selected, it pastes nothing. What's pasted is selected, with focus on it, or for a Row on the Selection bar's chip. A pasted Block, Row or text is one step for undo, and image files are added as dropped ones are. |

The browser's Edit menu does the same, and so do More's Copy, Cut and Paste below. Paste below reads the clipboard through the browser, which asks the person first, and if it says no, the editor says why. A paste goes where this table says.

| What's pasted | With a Block selected | With a Row selected | With nothing selected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Block copied in a Mail Quilt editor | After it, in its Column | In a new Row after it | In a new Row at the end |
| A Row copied in a Mail Quilt editor | After the Block's Row | After it | At the end |
| Text or HTML from another app | A new Text Block after it | A new Text Block in a new Row after it | A new Text Block in a new Row at the end |
| An image file | A new Image Block after it | A new Image Block in a new Row after it | A new Image Block in a new Row at the end |

A Block or Row pasted from a Mail Quilt editor, in this email, another one, or another tab, keeps every setting, and gets new IDs. Pasted anywhere else, such as in a document, it's text and simple HTML: text keeps its formatting and Lists, an Image is an image, and a Button is a link. Text or HTML from another app keeps only what a Text Block can hold, as when it's pasted into text, and each `{{name}}` in it becomes a Merge Tag. An image file uploads as a dropped one does, as [Upload images](./upload-images#pasting-an-image) says. Without `uploadImage` it's refused, and screen readers hear that the editor can't upload images. A copy from a newer version of the editor is refused, and screen readers hear why, as they do when nothing in what's copied can go in the email, such as only spaces.

In a Block's text or a settings field, these keys copy, cut and paste text as usual. A Block pasted into text gives its text.

## Adding a Block

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>/</kbd> | On a Block, in the Selection bar outside its fields, on a Row in the Rows list, on an empty Column's button, or in the toolbar below the email, opens the Add panel with focus in its search. In a Block's text or a field, it types a slash. On a keyboard where <kbd>/</kbd> needs Shift, such as a German one, <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>/</kbd> works too. |

The Add panel has every type of Block, then the layouts of an empty Row, then your app's [Saved Rows](./api#saved-rows), if it offers any. Type in its search to show only the ones whose name has what you typed. <kbd>Down</kbd> goes from the search to the first Block, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds the first one that's shown. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> in the search undoes the email, so you can take back what you just added. The Blocks are one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, the empty Rows another, and the Saved Rows a third: the arrow keys go along them, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last.

<kbd>Enter</kbd> on a Block adds it after the selected Block, in the same
Column. With a Row selected instead, it goes to the end of that Row's first
Column, and with nothing selected, to the end of the email's last Column. The
new Block is selected, with focus in its text if it has any, and screen readers
hear its type and where it is, such as "Text Block added, Row 3, Column 1,
position 2 of 3." Where the panel sits beside the email, it stays open, so you
can add another. Where it opens over the email, it closes, so you can see what
you added.
<kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it, and focus goes to the selected Block, or back to
where it was. The Add button at the end of the Selection bar, with nothing
selected, opens it too.

An empty Column has a button named for where it is, such as "Add a Block, or drop one here, in Row 1, Column 2", one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>. <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens a picker in the Column with focus on its first Block. The arrow keys go along them, <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds one to the Column, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes the picker and goes back to the button.

With a mouse, rest the pointer on the edge between two Blocks, and a line with a + shows there. The + opens the same picker there, with a search, and it adds below the Block above it. On a touch screen there's no +, so use the Add panel or an empty Column's button.

## Adding a Row

In the Add panel or the Rows list's +, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on a layout to add an empty Row with it, or on a Saved Row to add a copy of it, after the selected Row, or after the Row of the selected Block. With nothing selected, it goes at the end of the email. The new Row is selected, with focus on the Selection bar's chip.

## Settings

A settings field, such as a Button's link or a Row's padding, changes the email when you press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or leave the field. Until then, <kbd>Escape</kbd> takes back what you typed and shows the email's value again. A value the editor refuses stays in the field with the reason, until you fix it or press <kbd>Escape</kbd>. With nothing typed, <kbd>Escape</kbd> in a popover's field or More's closes it, and in a field in the Selection bar it's left to your page. A choice of a few, such as an alignment, is a row of buttons in the Selection bar, and pressing one sets it.

In the Phone view, a field that can have a [Phone value](./document-format#phone-values), a font size, padding or an Image's width, changes that, and a field with one is marked with a phone. In the Selection bar, a card under it says both values, and its Use the desktop value button, which the arrow keys reach after the field, clears the Phone value. Elsewhere, the ✕ in the field is that button.

A Social Block's links are a list in its Links popover, each with its URL field and a toolbar of Move up, Move down and Remove. The toolbar comes before the URL, and like the Selection bar it's one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>: the arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to its first and last. After a move, focus stays on the button that moved the link. After a removal, it goes to the Remove button of the next link, or of the one before if it was the last, or to the list of networks if none is left. To add a link, choose a network that isn't in the list yet and press Add, and focus goes to the new link's URL. What's typed in a URL stays with its link when links move or are removed.

A Menu's links are a list in its Links popover too, each a line of its label, its URL and the same toolbar, in that order, each a stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>. A new Menu's Selection bar has Add link in place of Links, which adds a link and opens the popover with focus in its label, as the popover's own Add link does. After a removal of the only link, focus goes to Add link. Otherwise focus goes as it does for a Social Block's links, and what's typed stays with its link.

A Video's Play button opens its three choices, each a line with what it means, with focus on the one chosen. They're one stop for <kbd>Tab</kbd>, as a group of radio buttons is: the arrow keys go to the next or previous and choose it, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the Play button. Going past "On the picture" with the arrow keys draws nothing, as drawing starts only after a moment on it. It's chosen once the play button is drawn and uploaded, and until then the editor says "Adding the play button…". Without the Host app's image uploads it's skipped, and says why. Before a Video's link is pasted, its Thumbnail button is off, and the arrow keys still reach it, as they do every control in the Selection bar, so its tooltip can say why.

## Style

Style shows the email beside its Email style. The email there is only to look at: nothing in it takes focus or is typed in, and it isn't read out, as Edit has it to read and change. A press on a Block selects it, and the panel then says each value the Block keeps as its own, with a button to make it match the rest and one to give every Block like it the same value.

Each setting in the panel is a button that says its value, or "Default" and what that is. Focus on one, or the pointer over it, marks on the email what it changes, and what keeps a value of its own. <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens what picks its value, with focus in it and its text selected to type over, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes that and goes back to the setting. <kbd>Tab</kbd> from its last control closes it and goes on to the next setting, and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> from its first goes back to its own. A press outside it closes it too. Button corners are a group of three choices, which the arrow keys go along and choose.

A color opens with focus in its Hex field, where a color typed is set with <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Before it, the shade and the hue are picked with the pointer or the keys, and the email changes as they move.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | In the shade, makes the color less or more saturated. |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | In the shade, makes the color brighter or darker. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | In the shade, the same, ten times as far. |

The keys pressed in the shade at one time are one step for undo, as is one drag across it.

A font opens with focus in its search, which shows only the fonts whose name has what you typed.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | In a font's search, goes along the fonts that are shown. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | In a font's search, picks the font the arrow keys are on, closes the list and goes back to the setting. |

A size opens with focus in its field. Each setting changed, and each value cleared with "Use the default", is one step for undo. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> undoes the email from anywhere in the panel.

## Cancelling a drag

While a Block or Row is dragged with the pointer, including a new one from the Add panel, <kbd>Escape</kbd> cancels the drag. Nothing moves and nothing is added. Until the drag ends, the keys that select or move a Block or Row, or go into a Block's text, do nothing.

## Editing text

<kbd>⌘</kbd> is for a Mac, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> for everything else. On a Mac,
Ctrl+K and Ctrl+Y keep their usual meaning in text: delete to the end of the
line, and put it back.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Starts a new paragraph. In a List's item, it starts a new item, and in an empty item it leaves the List, splitting it if the item was in the middle. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Adds a line break in the same paragraph or item. |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Deletes backward. At the start of a List's item, it makes the item a paragraph, above the List if it was the first item and otherwise splitting the List. At the start of the paragraph just after a List, it joins the paragraph to the List's last item. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd> do the same, and on a Mac so do <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd> and <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>. |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> | Deletes forward. In an empty paragraph, it removes the paragraph, even the last one in the Block, and in an empty item it removes the item. At the end of a List's last item, or of the paragraph before a List, it pulls in the next paragraph's text, or the next List's first item, and that List keeps its type. When a paragraph between two Lists of the same type goes, with either key, they become one List, numbered on. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> does the same, and on a Mac so do <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Delete</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Also adds a line break. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>B</kbd> | Turns bold on or off for the selected text. A Heading is bold already, so it does nothing there. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>B</kbd> does the same. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd> | Turns italic on or off. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>I</kbd> does the same. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>U</kbd> | Turns underline on or off. <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>U</kbd> does the same, so that on Windows and Linux the browser doesn't format the text in its own way. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>8</kbd> | Makes each selected paragraph an item of a bulleted List, in a Text Block. In a bulleted List already, it makes the selected items paragraphs again, and in a numbered List it makes the whole List bulleted. It's the key with 8 on it, even on a keyboard where Shift with it types something other than `*`. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>7</kbd> | The same for a numbered List. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> | Opens the link field for the selected Block's text, from the text, the Block or a button in the Selection bar. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes it and leaves the text as it was. With no text to link, such as on a Button, the key is left to your page. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>F10</kbd> | Goes to the formatting toolbar, for bold, italic, underline, bulleted and numbered Lists, links, line breaks and, when your app gives the editor its Merge Tags, the Merge Tag button. The arrow keys go across its buttons, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last. <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the text, once it has closed the tooltip of the button with focus. |

## Picking a Merge Tag

When your app gives the editor its [Merge Tags](./api#merge-tags), typing `{{` in a Block's text opens a list of them at the cursor, and what's typed after `{{` narrows it. The format bar's Merge Tag button types `{{` for you. Focus stays in the text, and a screen reader is told the highlighted tag as it changes. Typing `{{` in a settings field that holds text or a URL, or in the link field, opens the same list under the field, with the same keys.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> | Highlights the tag before or after, going round from either end. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Puts in the highlighted tag, in place of the `{{` and what was typed after it. |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Closes the list and leaves the text as typed. The next <kbd>Escape</kbd> comes back out of the text to the Block, takes back what was typed in a settings field, or closes the link field. |

In a field, <kbd>Enter</kbd> puts in the tag as the text `{{name}}` without applying the field, and the next <kbd>Enter</kbd> applies it. Every other key does what it does in text, or in the field, so typing goes on. In text, typing `}}` makes a tag of what was typed, listed or not. Leaving the text or the field closes the list too.

## Undo and redo

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Undoes the last change. |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> | Redoes it. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Y</kbd> | Also redoes it, except on a Mac. |

They work in every Mode, as the Undo and Redo buttons in the top bar do, anywhere in the editor but these. In a settings field or the link field, they undo what's typed there, and the link field keeps them from your page. On the Shell's name, More and Export, they do nothing. With nothing to undo or redo, these keys are left to your page.

## Keys and your page

The keys the editor acts on stop in the editor, so your page doesn't act on them too. Every other key goes on to your page, including what's typed in a Block's text or a settings field, as from any field on a page. The link field is the one exception: it keeps every key to itself.

The editor is inside a shadow root, so your page sees a key pressed in it as coming from the `<mail-quilt>` element, or from `<mail-quilt-shell>` in the Shell. A shortcut of your page's own, such as a letter that opens search, can skip those keys:

```js
document.addEventListener("keydown", (event) => {
  if (event.target.closest("mail-quilt, mail-quilt-shell")) {
    return;
  }
  // Your page's own shortcuts.
});
```

## Check

Check is a Mode, reached like the others from the Modes in the top bar, or from the button below the email in Edit. Focus stays on its tab. From there, <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes to Check's tabs, Inbox, Plain text and HTML, which are one stop: the arrow keys go to the next or previous one and show it, and <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> the first or last. Then it goes to Show sample values, when your Merge Tags have samples and the tab isn't HTML, then through the email at each width, which is a page of its own with its links, or in Plain text and HTML to the Copy button and the text, which the arrow keys scroll, and then to Send a test, when your app sends tests, and the fix button of each Problem in Before you export. Send a test keeps focus while it says it's sending, and the result is read out. A fix button opens Edit with what the Problem is about selected, and moves focus to the field that fixes it, opening the popover or More it's in, with the Problem shown under the field.

<kbd>Escape</kbd> does nothing of Check's own, so it reaches your page. The
editor hears no key pressed inside the email, but <kbd>Alt</kbd> +
<kbd>Left</kbd> there still doesn't go back a page, except in Safari, which runs
no listener inside the email.

## What a screen reader announces

- **A Block's place.** Each Block's name says where it is, such as "Text Block, Row 1, Column 2, position 1 of 3".
- **Every move.** After a Block or Row moves, the editor says where it went. When one can't go further, it says that instead.
- **Undo and redo of a move.** These say where the Block went back to.
- **Duplicating.** After a Block or Row is duplicated, the editor says where the copy went, such as "Block duplicated, Row 1, Column 1, position 2 of 3".
- **Copying, cutting and pasting.** The editor says what was copied or cut and where it was, or where what was pasted went, such as "Block pasted, Row 2, Column 1, position 3 of 3". A paste it refuses says why.
- **A Social Block's or a Menu's links.** After a link is added, moved or removed in the settings, the editor says so, such as "Facebook moved to position 2 of 3". A Menu's link is named by its label, or until it has one, by its place, such as "Link 3 added, position 3 of 3".
- **Deleting.** After a Block or Row is deleted, the editor says so. Focus moves to the Block after it, or before it if it was the last. For a Row, the next Row is selected, or the previous one, and focus stays in the Selection bar.
- **Messages in the Shell.** The [Shell](./shell) announces each message in its bar, such as an email it couldn't open.

## How it's tested

The editor's browser tests press these keys in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. They also run [axe-core](https://github.com/dequelabs/axe-core) with its WCAG 2.2 AA rules against the editor in several states: in each Mode, with the Shell's Export menu open, with Columns and empty Columns, with a Block selected, with the Selection bar's popover, its chip's menu and More open, with the link field showing an error, with the Merge Tag picker open in text and under a settings field, with a Social Block's and a Menu's links showing a refused URL, with a new Video, and with a Video's play button choices open, one of them off, and in Check, with Problems to fix and with none, and in its Plain text and HTML tabs.

Automated checks don't find everything. The [accessibility statement](./accessibility) says which WCAG 2.2 criteria the editor meets, what it doesn't, and how each was checked. If a screen reader or another tool has trouble with the editor, please [open an issue](https://github.com/mailquilt/editor/issues).
