Every client renders email in its own way, so send yourself a test of your own emails before you rely on them. To report how an email looks in a client, open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/mailquilt/editor/issues).

## Classic Outlook for Windows

Classic Outlook draws email with Microsoft Word, which supports less HTML and CSS than a browser. Columns, buttons, images, text and spacing come out as designed, but emoji are black and white, and a flag shows as its two letters, such as "IN". A List's bullets and numbers sit at the paragraph's edge in Outlook 2016 and 2019, and further in than in other clients in later versions, and a number of two digits, such as "10.", touches its text. Its dark mode also inverts colors, as [below](#dark-mode-in-gmail-outlook-and-apple-mail).

## Rows on phones

On screens under 480px wide, a Row's Columns go one under another. That comes from the email's styles, so a client that drops them on a phone, such as the Gmail app with an account that isn't Google's, keeps its Columns at their desktop width, wrapping onto as many lines as they need, such as two Columns of a four-Column Row on each line. A Row set to keep its Columns side by side on phones has their widths written on each Column, so it looks the same in every client.

## Showing on a device and Phone values

[Showing a Block or Row on one device](./document-format#showing-on-a-device) and [Phone values](./document-format#phone-values) are rules in the email's styles for screens under 480px wide, as hiding a Row on phones was in version 0.3. Version 0.4 adds them, and they haven't had an Email on Acid run yet; this says what the HTML asks each client to do.

- **Desktop only** is shown, and hidden on a phone by the email's styles. A client that drops them on a phone shows it there too.
- **Phones only** is hidden in the HTML itself, and shown on a phone by the email's styles. A client that drops them leaves it out on a phone too, so put nothing there that a reader must see. Classic Outlook for Windows reads no media query, so the HTML leaves it out for Outlook altogether.
- **Phone values** for font size, padding and an Image's width take the place of the desktop's on a phone, from the email's styles. A client that drops them shows the desktop's values on a phone.

## Boxes and Columns

A Block's [Box](./document-format#box) and a [Column](./document-format#column)'s settings are written on the table cell around the Block or Column, which every client above reads, Outlook too. Version 0.4 adds them, and they haven't had an Email on Acid run yet; this says what the HTML asks each client to draw.

- **Padding, borders and a Column's alignment** are plain cell styles, which classic Outlook for Windows draws as set.
- **Background colors** are drawn as set when they're solid. Classic Outlook for Windows doesn't support see-through colors written as `rgba()`, so it may show no background for one. Use a solid color, such as `#efe6da`, for a background Outlook readers must see.
- **Corners** are `border-radius`, which classic Outlook for Windows doesn't support, so it draws a Block's corners square, and a Button's too. The background and border still show, in a square box.
- **A Column's background** reaches only as far down as its Blocks, in every client, so two Columns of different heights have backgrounds of different heights. Keep a Column's background for a Column as tall as its Row, or give the Row the color instead.

## Web fonts

An email that uses one of your [web fonts](./api#web-fonts) loads it in the email clients that support web fonts, and shows its fallback in the rest. Version 0.4 adds them, and they haven't had an Email on Acid run yet. This is what [Can I email](https://www.caniemail.com/features/css-at-font-face/) found when it last tested them, in December 2023:

- **Loads the web font:** Apple Mail on macOS, iOS Mail, Samsung Email on Android except with a Microsoft account such as Outlook or Hotmail, Thunderbird on macOS, and the GMX and WEB.DE apps on iOS.
- **Shows the fallback:** Gmail on the web, Android and iPhone, Outlook.com, new Outlook for Windows, Outlook for Mac, iOS and Android, Yahoo Mail and AOL.
- **Classic Outlook for Windows** never loads a web font. The export hides the font's stylesheet from it, as Can I email says Outlook 2007 to 2016 ignore the font list and use Times New Roman for a font declared in one, so it reads the font list and shows the fallback.

So pick a fallback close to the web font, and check your email with it: most of your readers may see it.

## URLs and long words

A URL written as text, starting with `https://`, `http://` or `www.`, is exported as a link to itself, in its text's color, like a link you add in the editor. One starting with `www.` links to `http://` and that address. An email address written as text is exported as a `mailto:` link the same way. The canvas still shows both as text. A URL ends before a closing bracket it didn't open, and before a full stop or comma after it, so `(see https://example.com/a_(b))` links `https://example.com/a_(b)`. A URL or address inside a Merge Tag, such as its default value, stays part of the tag. An address may have letters in any language, as in `müller@example.com`. A language written without spaces between words, such as Japanese, ends a URL or address, so `info@example.comまで` links `info@example.com`. So does its full-width punctuation, such as `，` or `（`. A URL with such letters typed in its path, as in `https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京`, links only up to them. Paste it as your browser copies it, percent-encoded, or link it yourself. A URL or address whose formatting changes partway, such as a bold word in it, links whole. One that runs into a link you added, or straight into a character an address can't hold, as in `a@b.c_d`, isn't linked.

A word of 20 or more characters as typed, such as that URL, gets invisible break points after its slashes, dots and other punctuation, in text and in a Button's label, so it wraps onto the next line in every client rather than widening the email. It's measured whole, even when part of it is bold or otherwise formatted. Someone who copies it from the email copies the break points too. A link's address is never changed, and Merge Tags are left whole, with or without spaces inside their braces.

Apple Mail, iOS Mail and Gmail also turn text that looks like an address without `https://`, such as `example.com`, into a link in their own blue. To keep it in your colors, link it yourself.

## Dark mode in Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail

In dark mode, clients do one of three things to an email:

| What happens | Clients |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing. The email looks as designed | Apple Mail, iOS Mail, Gmail in a browser, AOL |
| Light backgrounds are darkened, and dark text lightened | Gmail app on Android, Outlook.com, new Outlook, Outlook for Mac, Yahoo |
| Every color is inverted, dark Rows and buttons included | Classic Outlook for Windows, Gmail app on iOS |

Nothing in an email can turn this off in classic Outlook or the Gmail app on iOS. What helps is choosing colors that read either way:

- **Buttons.** A button that's clearly dark, such as navy or black, or clearly light, stays readable when it's inverted. A middle color, such as green or orange, can end up with text in nearly its own color: in the test, Yahoo turned the dark text on an orange button pale, and the Gmail app on iOS turned the white text on a green button dark.
- **Images.** Most clients don't change images, but in dark mode the Gmail app on Android recolors them: it turned white icons black and made some colored ones lighter. A logo drawn on its own dark background shows as a dark box on a Row that was inverted to light. Use a PNG with a transparent background, in a color that reads on light and dark. [Social icons](./social-icons#dark-mode) says which of its icons show where.
- **Meaning.** Don't let color alone carry meaning, such as red for a warning, since it may not stay red.

## Gmail clipping at 102 KB

Gmail cuts off an email it counts as over 102 KB. It shows the start, then a "View entire message" link, so the end of the email, often with the unsubscribe link, is hidden. Images don't count, as the HTML only links to them.

The Gmail app on Android sometimes shows "View entire message" below an email that's already complete. That isn't clipping, and nothing is missing.

The export leaves out MJML's indentation to stay small: the test email, with 10 Rows, is about 42 KB. What Gmail counts can be more than the HTML, such as what your sending service adds for tracking, so keep well under the limit. Check an email's size before you send it:

```js
const bytes = new TextEncoder().encode(html).length;
```

## Social icons

A Social Block writes each icon as a PNG, linked, with the network's name as its alt text, because Gmail and Outlook drop SVG and `data:` images. Each icon loads from jsDelivr, or from your own storage with [`socialIconsUrl`](./social-icons), when the email is opened. A client that blocks images shows the alt text, or nothing, as below.

## Images turned off

Some clients block images until the reader allows them. AOL then shows a broken-image icon without the alt text, and the image takes up no space. Make sure the email still makes sense without its images, and don't put text the reader needs only in an image.
