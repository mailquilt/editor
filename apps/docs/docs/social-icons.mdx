A Social Block shows a row of linked icons, for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky and a website. Each icon is the brand's own mark, in its own color or in white for a dark background, at 32, 40 or 48 pixels. The Selection bar sets the style, the size and the alignment, and its Links button opens the links. A person adds a network that isn't in the list yet, types its URL, and moves it up or down or removes it, with buttons that work from the [keyboard](./keyboard#settings). A link without a URL shows faded on the canvas and isn't sent. In code, the links are set with the [`updateBlockProps`](./change-emails-in-code#commands) Command, as the [document format](./document-format#social) describes.

## Where the icons load from

An email can't carry its icons inside it, as Gmail and Outlook drop SVG and `data:` images. So each icon is a PNG at a public URL, and an email client loads it when the email is opened.

The icons are files in `@mailquilt/renderer`, in its `icons` folder, and by default an email loads them from [jsDelivr](https://www.jsdelivr.com), which serves every npm package. The URL names the renderer's exact version:

```text
https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mailquilt/renderer@0.3.0/icons/facebook-color.png
```

A URL with an exact version always names the same file, so a later release can't change an email you've already sent. The canvas and Preview load the same URLs as the export, so what you see is what's sent.

That means a sent email loads its icons from jsDelivr, a third party, for as long as anyone opens it. Neither npm nor jsDelivr promises to serve a file forever. If your emails need that, or should load nothing from a third party, serve the icons yourself.

## Serve the icons yourself

Copy the renderer's `icons` folder to your own storage, such as a bucket behind your CDN, and give its URL as `socialIconsUrl`. The slash at the end is optional.

```js
// On your server
const html = await renderEmail(email, {
  socialIconsUrl: "https://cdn.example.com/mail-icons/",
});

// In the browser, for the canvas, Preview and exportHtml()
editor.socialIconsUrl = "https://cdn.example.com/mail-icons/";
```

`<mail-quilt-shell>` takes the same property, and passes it to the editor inside. In React, `MailQuilt` and `MailQuiltShell` take it as a prop:

```tsx
<MailQuilt
  onChange={setEmail}
  socialIconsUrl="https://cdn.example.com/mail-icons/"
  value={email}
/>
```

The folder is in `node_modules/@mailquilt/renderer/icons`. Copy it again when you update the renderer, as a release can add an icon or change one, and keep its `NOTICE.md` with the icons.

## Content Security Policy

The canvas and Preview show the icons from their URLs. If your page's policy has `img-src`, or a `default-src` without one, allow jsDelivr, or set `socialIconsUrl` and allow your own storage instead:

```text
Content-Security-Policy: img-src 'self' https://cdn.jsdelivr.net
```

An icon the policy blocks doesn't show on the canvas or in Preview. The exported HTML is the same either way.

## Trademarks and brand guidelines

The logos are trademarks of their owners. Mail Quilt isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by any of them. When a brand's owner asks, its icon is removed from the next release. That changes the emails rendered after it, and emails already sent keep loading the icon from the version they name.

The person sending the email follows each brand's guidelines for its logo:

- [Facebook](https://www.meta.com/brand/resources/facebook/logo/)
- [Instagram](https://www.meta.com/brand/resources/instagram/instagram-brand/)
- [LinkedIn](https://brand.linkedin.com/policies)
- [X](https://about.x.com/en/who-we-are/brand-toolkit)
- [YouTube](https://brand.youtube/)
- [TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/getting-started-design-guidelines)
- [Pinterest](https://business.pinterest.com/en/brand-guidelines)
- [Threads](https://www.meta.com/brand/resources/instagram/threads/)
- [Bluesky](https://bsky.social/about/blog/press-faq)

Mail Quilt draws each icon as its brand shapes it, with no circle or other shape of its own around it, as Facebook, LinkedIn and Bluesky ask. The exceptions are X, Threads and TikTok in color, which are white on a black rounded square, as X asks for its logo white on black or black on white, and the website icon, which is white on dark grey. Drawn black on their own, they would disappear in dark mode. The color icons keep the parts a brand draws in white, such as Facebook's "f", white on any background. The gap between icons is half their size, which gives Instagram and Facebook the clear space they ask for. It doesn't give Bluesky its clear space, which is the logo's own size. YouTube asks for a logo at least 100 pixels tall, which may apply to its icon too, and the largest icon is 48.

## Dark mode

Many email apps change an email's colors in dark mode, as [Email clients](./email-clients#dark-mode-in-gmail-outlook-and-apple-mail) describes, and none of them can be told how to change an image. So each icon carries what it needs to be seen:

- **Color icons show on light and dark backgrounds.** Each is in its brand's color with its white parts, or white on a tile of its color, so it reads on a white Row that dark mode turns near-black, and on a dark Row. On a Row in a brand's own color, such as Bluesky's blue, that brand's icon can blend in, so check how a colored Row looks.
- **White icons need the dark background they were chosen for.** Classic Outlook for Windows and the Gmail app on iPhone make a dark Row light in dark mode, and the Gmail app on Android turns the icons dark. In all three, white icons are hard to see. Nothing in the email can stop it without outlining or reshaping the logos, which Facebook and Pinterest don't allow. Where those apps matter, use color icons.
