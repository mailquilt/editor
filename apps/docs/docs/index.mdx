Mail Quilt is {{package-count}} npm packages for adding a drag-and-drop email editor to a web app. They're free and MIT-licensed, and they run in your app, with no service to sign up for.

## What it is

There are three ways in, from the most done for you to the least:

- **The Shell** is a whole editor page, ready-made. You build nothing. The [Playground](/playground) is one.
- **The editor** is the `<mail-quilt>` element, or `MailQuilt` in React: the canvas, the Rows list, its Selection bar and its Add panel. You build your own page around it.
- **The core and the renderer** are the email as data, the Commands that change it, and the HTML. The editor is built on them, and you can [build on them too](./change-emails-in-code).

Most of these docs are about the editor, because that's where most apps start. [Packages](./packages) says what each one is for.

## What people can do in the editor

Add `<mail-quilt>` to a page, and the people using your app can design emails without writing HTML:

- Build an email from {{block-names}} Blocks.
- Lay it out in Rows of one to {{most-columns}} Columns.
- Add Blocks where they're working, from the + between two Blocks or the Add panel, drag them in from the panel, drag them to reorder them or move them to another Column, and drag Rows to reorder them, on the email or in the Rows list, which shows each Row as a small picture. Everything they can do by dragging, they can also do with the keyboard.
- Make text bold, italic or underlined, add links, and write Merge Tags such as `{{first_name}}`.
- Change the settings of each Block and Row, and of the email as a whole.
- Undo and redo every change.
- Check the exact HTML the email will be sent as, at a desktop's width and a phone's, with a list of what to fix before it's exported, such as an Image with no alt text or a Button with no link.

To try it, open the [Playground](/playground). There's nothing to install.

## How it works

Your app keeps the emails. The editor works on a JSON document, and after every change it hands you the new one. Save it wherever you like, such as in your database, and give it back to the editor to open the email again. The [document format](./document-format) page describes it.

When an email is ready, the editor turns it into HTML with [MJML](https://mjml.io), which writes the table-based markup that email clients need. Merge Tags stay in the HTML as written, so the service that sends your email can fill them in.

The editor doesn't send email, store emails for you, or run a server. Its packages live in your app.

## Add it to your app

- **Any framework, or none:** `@mailquilt/editor` registers the `<mail-quilt>` element. Load it from a CDN on a page with no build step, as [HTML, no build step](./html) shows, or through your bundler, as the [quick start](./quick-start) shows. [Vue, Angular and Svelte](./other-frameworks) use the same element.
- **React:** `@mailquilt/react` wraps it as a `MailQuilt` component, which also works with server rendering. See [React](./react) and [Next.js](./nextjs).

[Packages](./packages) says what the other two packages are for. Wrappers for Vue and Svelte will follow if the element alone isn't enough. To match the editor to your app's colors and font, set its [theme tokens](./theme-tokens).

## Before you rely on it

- Mail Quilt is at version `0.x`, so its API can still change between releases.
- Classic Outlook for Windows shows the exported HTML with a few limits. The [tested email clients](./email-clients) page lists them, and every client it was checked in.
