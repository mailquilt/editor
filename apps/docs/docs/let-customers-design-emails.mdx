If your app sends email for its customers, such as receipts, reminders or newsletters, sooner or later they'll want to design those emails themselves. This page shows how Mail Quilt fits that: where the editor goes, where each email is kept, how Merge Tags reach the service that sends, and what to tell customers the editor can't do yet.

## How the pieces fit

1. **A customer designs the email** in the editor, on a page of your app.
2. **Your app keeps it.** After every change, the editor hands your app the email as a JSON document. Save it with the rest of the customer's data. Mail Quilt stores nothing and runs no server.
3. **Your server makes the HTML** from the JSON when it's time to send, with the [renderer](./render-on-a-server).
4. **Your sending service fills in the Merge Tags** and sends it.

Mail Quilt adds no service to that path. There's no account or API key to set up, and the packages run in your app and on your server.

## Put the editor in your app

Choose the [Shell](./shell) for a whole editor page, with Export and Import buttons and messages, or put the `<mail-quilt>` editor in a page of your own. [Fit the editor into your page](./design-your-editor-page#shell-or-your-own-page) helps you choose.

- **Make it look like your app.** The editor's colors, font, spacing and corners are [theme tokens](./theme-the-editor), so customers see your brand around the email. The email itself keeps the colors and font the customer chose for it.
- **It's white-label as it comes.** The editor shows no Mail Quilt name or logo, and neither does the HTML it exports.
- **Give it room.** The editor works best at a laptop's width or more. Below {{editor-phone-width}} pixels, as on a phone, it's laid out for one, with the Selection bar at the bottom and its panels as sheets, so customers can fix a typo or a link away from their desk. The [width](./design-your-editor-page#width) section has the details.
- **It works from the keyboard.** Anything a customer can do by dragging, they can also do from the keyboard. [Keyboard and screen readers](./keyboard) lists the keys and what a screen reader announces.

## Keep each email with the customer

Save the JSON from the editor's change event, keyed however your app keys a customer's emails. [Save emails to your server](./save-emails) shows the code, and the [document format](./document-format) page describes the JSON.

- **Upgrading doesn't strand saved emails.** Each email records the version that saved it, and a newer Mail Quilt brings an older email up to date when it loads it.
- **The editor refuses what it can't trust.** Given an email it can't read, it changes nothing and tells your app why, so an auto-save can't replace a customer's email with a guess.

## Give customers somewhere to start

Mail Quilt has no template library, so the starter emails are yours to make, which also means they can match your app. Design each one in the [Playground](/playground), or in a [Shell](./shell) in your app, and keep what its Export JSON button gives you. When a customer creates an email, give the editor your starter emails as [`starterEmails`](./api#starter-emails) and an email with no Rows as its `value`, and a start screen lets them pick one or start blank. To skip the choice, pass one starter email as `value` instead.

The parts your customers reuse, such as your footer, can be [Saved Rows](./api#saved-rows): Rows from an email you designed the same way, which the editor offers wherever a Row is added.

To make starter emails in code instead, such as one per customer with their name already in it, use [Commands](./change-emails-in-code) from the core package.

## Merge Tags

Customers type Merge Tags such as `{{first_name}}` anywhere in text, and in any setting that holds text or a URL, such as a link to `{{unsubscribe_url}}`. Mail Quilt keeps them in the HTML, so the service that sends your email can fill them in.

Give the editor your app's tags with [`mergeTags`](./api#merge-tags), and customers pick one from a list when they type `{{` in text or a setting, with the labels you give, such as "First name".

- **Tell customers which tags work.** Mail Quilt doesn't check a tag's name, so it can't warn a customer about a tag your sending service doesn't know.
- **Keep tags to names.** An `&`, `<` or `>` inside a tag is escaped, and a `"` too in a link or image URL or alt text, as [Merge Tags](./document-format#merge-tags) explains, so your sending service would see the escaped text.

[Send with your email service](./send-email) shows how to fill tags on your server instead, and which services escape the values they fill.

## Images

Customers add an image by its URL, which starts with `https:` or `http:`, or with a Merge Tag. To let them upload one instead, give the editor a function that sends the file to storage your app already has and returns its URL. [Upload images](./upload-images) shows one, with a pre-signed upload.

## Render at send time

Keep the JSON as the email, and make the HTML when you send, with `renderEmail()` from `@mailquilt/renderer` on your server. It runs in Node 22 or later and loads no editor code. Rendering at send time means each email is rendered by the renderer you have installed that day, and your server never trusts HTML that came from a browser. [Render on a server](./render-on-a-server) has the code, and what happens when the renderer refuses an email.

## Before you offer it to customers

- **The editor has {{block-count}} Blocks:** {{block-names}}, in Rows of one to {{most-columns}} Columns.
- **Mail Quilt is at version `0.x`,** so its API can still change between releases. Saved emails are brought up to date when they load.
- **Classic Outlook for Windows shows the HTML with a few limits,** such as black-and-white emoji. [Tested email clients](./email-clients) lists every client it was checked in.
- **One person builds Mail Quilt,** in the open. [Why it exists](/why) says what that means for the roadmap.
